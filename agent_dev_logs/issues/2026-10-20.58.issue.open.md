Before the first runs:

- **API:** the candidates don't use OpenAI chat completions. All three serve `POST /v1/systemone` (Jev/SystemOne): SGLang's `dev-clef` image for Clef and Clef-Flash, `strands-decider serve` for Strands. One small client covers all three. It will live here, and ocr-postprocess, digital-convert and keyword-extract will keep pinned copies.
- **Clef runtime:** tested with torch 2.11 / transformers 5.10.2 on one H200 (bf16), so it gets its own environment. Inputs are capped at 16k tokens by default and 4 images per request, so page scans are downscaled.
- **Strands Decider 2B** reads text; images need its experimental `[vision]` extra (transformers ≥ 5.18). It fits OCR lines better than page scans.
- **Classifier:** with ~96 % top-1 already, the LDM only sees pages under a CNN confidence threshold, and chooses among the CNN's top 3. Routes are asked as yes/no per processor (OCR, HTR, tables, image description), because a page can need several. Their gold comes from the 11 labels.
- **ocr-postprocess:** shadow-mode decisions on the ambiguous lines (short lines, red flags without a conviction, the Noisy/Trash boundary), scored on the 2,067-line gold set with the existing adoption gate. The categories stay frozen until after the pilot.
- **digital-convert:** a type per page and per document, from the rendered page and its text, chosen from AMČR's `dokument_typ` list (32 of its 41 terms apply to PDFs). Can AMČR confirm that list? The catalogue type of each sample PDF would be the document-level gold.
- **keyword-extract:** the controlled category chosen by walking down the vocabulary (facet → group → the terms' own tree), with "none of these" at every step, as a fourth option for ufal/atrium-keyword-extract#2.

For David Spacil, two questions on track 2 from Opus, the structured description of archive images.

Two of its fields take one answer from a closed list: how the image was made, and the object-count pattern (1, N, 1a…1n). A decision model will answer them. It receives the image (and, optionally, the description of the linked document) and returns a probability for each listed option, so its answer is always one of them. Apart from the option names, it is told only a one-line description of each option.

1. **How the image was made:** during the dig, in a lab after the dig, other, or unknown. Does that cover the photographs and drawings in the archive, or is a value missing? A one-line description of each value would be what the model reads.
2. **Objects:** a vision-language model will name the objects on an image with vocabulary terms. Are Artefact, Material, Feature, Finds Context and Chronology the right facets to choose them from?

P.S.

Changes since 6 November, released in [`v1.14.2-beta`](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-page-classification/releases/tag/v1.14.2-beta):

- **Processors:** the model is no longer asked yes/no per processor. It answers which of seven elements a page holds: printed text, handwriting, a table or form, filled-in fields, a stamp, a photograph, a drawing. The OCR, HTR, tables and graphics routes are derived from these, and the model also names the one processor that matters most.
- **Page condition (new):** 15 yes/no defects of the paper, the ink and the scan, the text's orientation, and three scores from 0 (intact) to 4 (severe): paper, content and overall damage.
- **Synthetic gold:** `data_scripts/degrade_pages.py` makes graded copies of clean pages with six defects (skew, yellowing, faint ink, blur, cropping, uneven light) and three rotations. How the answer changes between a page and its copy measures the question without annotators.

`decide_pages.py --task triage` asks both in one request per page. Offline only; nothing has been run on a model yet.

#### <img src="https://avatars.githubusercontent.com/u/65828620?u=80b39d3cb2967c52e1d4af28dcefc7a1efdfdc79&v=4" width="50">[K4TEL](https://github.com/K4TEL) commented at [2026-11-08 17:29](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-page-classification/issues/58#issuecomment-6724698966):

**Decision models, briefly.** A decision model is a language model that chooses instead of writing. It gets an input (a page image, a text, or both) and questions whose answers are fixed in advance: yes/no, one option from a list, or a point on a scale. It returns a probability for every allowed answer, so it never answers off the list, though it can choose wrongly. It is not trained on our data: a short task description, the question and one line per answer are all it is told. One pass answers dozens of questions, so it is cheap, and the probabilities mark the cases it is unsure of, which a person or the existing tool can then decide.

Here it sees the page image only. Three question sets:

| Set | Asked per page | Answer | Checked against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category | which of the 11 categories; also as a second opinion where the classifier is unsure | 1 of 11 (1 of the classifier's top 3) | the dataset's labels |
| Content | what the page holds; which processor matters most | 7 yes/no; 1 of 4 | what the true category implies, e.g. a `TEXT_HW` page has handwriting and no print |
| Condition | defects, text orientation, damage | 15 yes/no; 1 of 5; three 0–4 scores | clean pages against their deliberately damaged copies |

Measured: how often it is right and what it confuses; for the second opinion, the pages it corrects against those it spoils; whether its confidence holds (answers given at 90 % should be right 9 times in 10); time per page and failed requests. Filled-in fields, stamps and real paper damage wait for annotated pages.

**First run** (`supplementary/scripts/decide_brief_job.sh`): a short zero-shot run on the cluster, with the existing 11-category dataset only: 20 pages per category from the fold-1 development pages, which the classifier was not trained on. It asks the category both ways, and content with condition in one request. The test pages wait until the questions are frozen. The run checks that the chain works and what a page costs; it does not yet say how good the model is.
